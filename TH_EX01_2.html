Visual Analytics for Data Discovery: Motor Insurance Portfolio Analysis

1 Introduction

Insurance companies often face challenges in identifying the underlying causes of poor profitability and high portfolio volatility. While traditional reporting tools provide summary-level insights, they may not fully reveal hidden patterns, risk drivers, or relationships within large and complex insurance datasets. As a result, insurers increasingly rely on visual analytics techniques to support more effective exploratory analysis and data-driven decision-making.

This assignment applies visual analytics methods to a real-world motor vehicle insurance portfolio dataset using R programming and relevant visualisation libraries. Through the use of statistical graphics and interactive visualisations, the analysis aims to explore numerical, categorical, and temporal patterns within the portfolio. The study focuses on identifying key factors that may influence insurance profitability, claim behaviour, and portfolio volatility.

By leveraging visual analytics techniques such as distribution analysis, comparative visualisations, correlation analysis, and multivariate exploration, this investigation seeks to uncover meaningful insights that can support underwriting decisions, portfolio segmentation, and risk management strategies. Ultimately, this assignment demonstrates how visual analytics can enhance understanding of insurance data beyond conventional dashboard reporting approaches.

2 Data Description

The dataset used in this assignment is a real-world motor vehicle insurance portfolio dataset containing policy-level information related to insurance coverage, claims experience, customer characteristics, and vehicle attributes. The dataset is intended to support exploratory visual analytics for identifying factors influencing portfolio profitability and volatility.

The data consists of both numerical and categorical variables collected across multiple insurance policies. Numerical variables include measures such as total premium, total incurred claims, total claims, total exposure, vehicle age, and driver age. These variables provide quantitative insights into policy performance, claims severity, and financial outcomes. Categorical variables include policy type, business type, fuel type, policy status, vehicle brand, and bonus score categories, which allow segmentation and comparative analysis across different customer and policy groups.

4.4 Feature Engineering

Feature engineering was performed to derive additional analytical variables from the raw insurance dataset. These engineered features, including loss ratio, claim frequency, claim severity, underwriting profit, profitability group, driver age group, high risk indicator, provide more meaningful indicators for evaluating portfolio profitability, claims behaviour, and risk exposure.

Here is an explanation to the various derived variables:

  • Loss Ratio: calculated as total incurred claims divided by total premium, representing portfolio profitability.

  • Claim Frequency: calculated as total claims divided by total exposure, representing how often the claims occurred.

  • Claim Severity: calculated as total incurred claims divided by total claims, representing the average cost per claim.

  • Underwriting Profit: calculated as total premium minus total incurred claims, representing the profitability of individual policies.

  • Profitability Group: categorised based on loss ratio thresholds, representing whether a policy is profitable, moderately profitable, or loss-making from an underwriting perspective.

  • Driver Age Group: categorised based on driver age ranges, representing different customer demographic segments for analysing claim behaviour and risk exposure.

  • High Risk Indicator: derived based on policies with loss ratios greater than 1, representing policies where incurred claims exceed collected premiums and may indicate underwriting concern.

motor_clean <- motor_clean %>%
  mutate(
    loss_ratio = ifelse(total_premium > 0,
                        total_incurred / total_premium,
                        NA),

    claim_frequency = ifelse(total_exposure > 0,
                             total_claims / total_exposure,
                             NA),

    claim_severity = ifelse(total_claims > 0,
                            total_incurred / total_claims,
                            NA),

    underwriting_profit =
      total_premium - total_incurred
  ) 
motor_clean <- motor_clean %>%
  mutate(

    # Profitability Group
    profitability_group = case_when(
      loss_ratio < 0.6 ~ "Profitable",
      loss_ratio < 1 ~ "Moderate",
      TRUE ~ "Loss Making"
    ),

    # Driver Age Group
    driver_age_group = case_when(
      driver_age < 25 ~ "Young",
      driver_age < 60 ~ "Adult",
      TRUE ~ "Senior"
    ),

    # High Risk Indicator
    high_risk = ifelse(
      loss_ratio > 1,
      "High Risk",
      "Normal"
    )

  )

8 Recommendations to Increase Profitability and Reduce Losses

Based on the visual analytics findings, several strategies may help improve portfolio profitability and reduce underwriting volatility.

Strengthen Underwriting Controls for High-Risk Segments

Policy segments such as COMP_N under business type P exhibited relatively high average loss ratios and may require closer underwriting review. The insurer could introduce stricter risk assessment criteria, tighter acceptance thresholds, or enhanced customer screening for these segments.

Improve Pricing and Risk-Based Premium Allocation

The strong relationship between incurred claims, claim severity, and underwriting losses suggests that certain high-risk policies may not be priced adequately relative to their expected claims exposure. Risk-based pricing models incorporating driver age, claims behaviour, and policy characteristics could help better align premiums with underlying risk levels.

Focus on Claim Severity Management

Since severe claims contribute disproportionately to underwriting losses, the insurer should prioritise claim severity management through fraud detection, early claims intervention, repair cost controls, and enhanced claims investigation procedures. Reducing large claim events may significantly improve overall portfolio profitability.

Enhance Portfolio Segmentation

The cluster analysis demonstrates that policyholders can be segmented into distinct behavioural and risk groups. Leveraging advanced segmentation techniques may allow the insurer to design more targeted underwriting, pricing, and retention strategies for different customer segments.

Monitor High-Risk Policies Continuously

Although high-risk policies form a relatively small proportion of the portfolio, they contribute disproportionately to volatility and losses. Continuous monitoring using visual analytics dashboards and predictive models may help identify deteriorating risk segments earlier and support proactive risk mitigation decisions.

9 Conclusion

This study demonstrates how visual analytics techniques can provide meaningful insights into the profitability and volatility of a motor insurance portfolio beyond conventional dashboard reporting. Through the application of univariate, bivariate, and multivariate visual analytics methods, the analysis successfully identified important patterns, relationships, and risk concentrations within the dataset.

The findings reveal that the portfolio is generally profitable overall, with most policies maintaining manageable loss ratios and stable underwriting performance. However, a smaller number of high-risk policies and severe claim events contribute disproportionately to underwriting volatility and financial losses. Variables such as claim severity, policy type, business type, and customer risk characteristics were found to significantly influence portfolio profitability.

These visual findings were further supported through statistical validation using regression analysis. The regression results confirmed that total incurred claims, loss ratio, claim frequency, and claim severity are statistically significant predictors of underwriting profit, with all variables demonstrating strong significance levels. In particular, total incurred claims and loss ratio exhibited the strongest negative impact on profitability, reinforcing the conclusion that claims-related factors are the primary drivers of underwriting volatility and financial performance.

The use of visual analytics techniques including heatmaps, treemaps, correlation analysis, pairwise relationship plots, clustering, and regression analysis enabled the identification of hidden relationships and high-risk portfolio segments that may not be easily observable through traditional reporting methods. These insights may support insurers in improving underwriting strategy, pricing optimisation, risk segmentation, and claims management practices, ultimately contributing to more sustainable portfolio profitability and enhanced risk management decisions.
